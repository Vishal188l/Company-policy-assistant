Company Policy Assistant

The same questions about a 98-policy database, answered three ways: rules-based keyword search, an LLM that reads every policy, and an LLM that first looks up the closest policies in a vector index.

Comparison and preferred approach

All three approaches were tested on the same 18 questions about a database of 98 company policies: 12 ordinary questions (half of them worded differently from the policy text), 2 trap questions where the policy lacks the detail being asked for, and 4 questions no policy covers. Rules-based keyword search answers instantly (under 1 ms) and costs no tokens, but it identified the right policy only 61% of the time. Whenever a question used different words from the policy, for example "my grandmother passed away" instead of "bereavement", it returned an unrelated policy, so a third of its answers were not supported by the database. Because it quotes policy text word for word, it never invents facts, but it confidently gives irrelevant ones. Both LLM approaches identified the correct policy for all 18 questions and correctly said "not covered" for every out-of-scope question. The LLM without a vector index reads all 98 policies for every question, which cost about 2,200 tokens and 8.7 seconds per answer, and in 2 of 18 answers (11%) it added advice the policies do not contain, such as telling the user to forward a journalist to "authorized personnel".

The LLM with a vector index is the preferred approach. It first retrieves the three policies closest in meaning to the question and sends only those to the model, so it used about 207 tokens per question (around 11 times fewer than sending the whole database), answered faster on average (6.8 seconds), matched the full-context LLM's 100% policy accuracy, and produced no unsupported answers. Giving the model less but more relevant text appears to leave it less room to embellish: on the sick-leave trap question it was the only approach to state explicitly that the policy gives no number of days. The cost savings also grow with the database, since the full-context approach must send every policy with every question while the indexed approach always sends three. The main caveats are that 18 questions is a small test set, response times on Gemini's free tier vary with rate limiting, and the hallucination check relies on an LLM judge. Within those limits, the vector-index approach gives the best balance of accuracy, honesty and cost, and it is the approach used by the Slack bot.
